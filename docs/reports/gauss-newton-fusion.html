1.2 What the iteration solves

ECC (Evangelidis & Psarakis, PAMI 2008) maximises the normalised correlation.

ρ(p) = ⟨ t‾ , i‾(p) ⟩ ‖t‾‖ ‖i‾(p)‖

It is a forward-additive Gauss-Newton iteration, and every step starts by building these three:

H= GTG vi= GTi‾ vt= GTt‾ H is the P×P Hessian (the Gram matrix). vi, vt is a vector of length P .

It then finds the scale λ that absorbs the illumination change,

λ= ‖i‾‖2 − viT H−1 vi ⟨t‾, i‾⟩ − vtT H−1 vi

and forms the parameter update.

Δp= H−1 GT ( λt‾ − i‾ )
There are GTfour multiplications by here: one in H (with itself), one each in vi and vt , and one in Δp . OpenCV implements them as four calls to project_onto_jacobian_ECC() . This report takes those four down to zero.

2. The current implementation, and what it costs

2.1 How the Jacobian is laid out

OpenCV keeps the Jacobian as a single hs × (ws*P) of size Mat -- P blocks side by side.

// modules/video/src/ecc.cpp
static void image_jacobian_affine_ECC(const Mat& src1, const Mat& src2,
                                      const Mat& src3, const Mat& src4, Mat& dst)
{
    const int w = src1.cols;
    dst.colRange(0, w)     = src1.mul(src3);  // gx * x
    dst.colRange(w, 2*w)   = src2.mul(src3);  // gy * x
    dst.colRange(2*w, 3*w) = src1.mul(src4);  // gx * y
    dst.colRange(3*w, 4*w) = src2.mul(src4);  // gy * y
    src1.copyTo(dst.colRange(4*w, 5*w));        // gx
    src2.copyTo(dst.colRange(5*w, 6*w));        // gy
}

The vector projections have the same shape: src2 (the image) and each block are read P times, so one projection is 12 planes , and there are three of them.

At a 768 px window one plane is 590K x 4 B = 2.25 MB, so that is 191 MB per iteration of memory traffic. And because Mat::dot has no parallel_for_ , all 85 of those planes stream on a single thread.

Measured, at window 768 on 12 threads this stage is 8.8 ms of a 16.0 ms iteration (55%) (those 8.8 ms cover 70 of the 85 planes -- the fourth projection and building error are not included in the 6.1 measurement, so the real share is a little higher). The two warps that the warp reduction targets are 1.12 ms (7%) under the same conditions.

3. Optimisation 1: the fourth projection falls out of the algebra

It is enough to expand the projection of the update.

GT ( λt‾ −i‾ ) = λ (GT t‾) − (GT i‾) = λvt −vi Plain linearity.vt and vi have already been computed.

The fourth project_onto_jacobian_ECC (12 planes) is unnecessary, and so is building the error matrix it consumes (3 planes). 85 planes become 70.

// before
error = lambda*templateZM - imageWarped;
project_onto_jacobian_ECC(jacobian, error, errorProjection);

// after
errorProjection = lambda*templateProjection - imageProjection;
This is a three-line substitution that changes no structure -- it touches neither the layout nor the algorithm. Accuracy is unchanged (the floating-point rounding differs, but skipping the intermediate error matrix removes an error source rather than adding one). It can go upstream on its own, and it helps every motion type.

4. Optimisation 2: the affine Hessian has only 18 degrees of freedom

4.1 The Jacobian is a face-splitting product

An affine warp is

x′= p0x+ p2y+ p4 y′= p1x+ p3y+ p5

so the six partial derivatives at pixel n are these:

∂i‾ ∂p = [ gxx gyx gxy gyy gx gy ] Left to right they correspond to p0 … p5 . The last two are the translation, so no coordinate multiplies them (they are multiplied by 1).

That is exactly the Kronecker product of the 2-vector gn with the 3-vector cn ,

row n of G = gn ⊗ cn , gn= (gx,gy) , cn= (x,y,1) 6 = 2 x 3. G is the face-splitting product (a row-wise Kronecker product) of the gradient matrix and the coordinate matrix.

So the Hessian takes this form.

H= GTG = ∑n (gn gnT) ⊗ (cn cnT) (a⊗b)(a⊗b)T =(aaT)⊗(bbT) applied at every pixel and summed, and nothing more.

gngnT is 2x2 symmetric with 3 independent entries (gx2, gxgy, gy2), and cncnT is 3x3 symmetric with 6 independent entries (x2, xy, y2, x, y, 1). Therefore:

The 21 upper-triangular entries of the 6x6 Hessian can be written with only 3 x 6 = 18 sums. The other three are duplicates (H12 = H03, H14 = H05, H34 = H25 -- all of them duplicates on the gxgy side). The affine ECC Hessian is not a general symmetric matrix.

4.2 The correspondence

Rows are the 3 entries of gngnT , columns the 6 entries of cncnT . Each cell names the Hessian entry it becomes.

x2 xy y2 x y 1
gx2 H₀₀H₀₂H₂₂H₀₄H₂₄H₄₄
gxgy H₀₁H₀₃ = H₁₂H₂₃H₀₅ = H₁₄H₂₅ = H₃₄H₄₅
gy2 H₁₁H₁₃H₃₃H₁₅H₃₅H₅₅

The projections have the same structure: entry GTv of (g,c) is ∑ngcv . There are no duplicates, 6 each, 12 for the two of them. Accumulate 30 scalars and this whole stage is done.

4.3 All four motion types have the same shape

This structure is not specific to affine. For every motion type, the ECC Jacobian can be written as a column subset of the face-splitting product of a gradient-side vector u with a coordinate-side vector w . What differs is the contents of u and w , and which columns are taken.

motion typeP u (gradient side)w (coordinate side) columnsH entriesscalars accumulated
translation2 (gx,gy) (1) 2 = 2x1 (all)33
euclidean3 (gr,gx,gy) (1) 3 = 3x1 (all)66
affine6 (gx,gy) (x,y,1) 6 = 2x3 (all)2118
homography8 (gx,gy,gp) (x,y,1) 8 = 3×3 − 13636

The number of scalars is always tri(K)·tri(L) (tri(n)=n(n+1)/2). Affine is the only one that genuinely gains, 18 < 21 , because 6 = 2x3 is the only case with K,L>1 that also uses every column. For the rest the counts coincide exactly.

But the real gain is not the smaller count -- it is never materialising the Jacobian . That takes one pass regardless of P , so the saving grows with P . The measurements in 6 come out exactly that way (window 768 on four threads: translation 1.27x rising to homography 2.47x).

What is gp for homography

The Jacobian of a projective warp carries a per-pixel division d=h6x+ h7y+1 . At first sight that breaks the face-splitting form, but it does not. Fold the division into the gradient side . OpenCV's own image_jacobian_homo_ECC already does exactly that:

gx=Ix/d , gy=Iy/d , gp= −x′gx −y′gy x′,y′ are the projected coordinates. hatX_ / hatY_ correspond to them.

Of the nine products of those three with (x,y,1) , the eight excluding gp·1 are the eight columns, unchanged.Far from being the exception, homography had the most headroom of all.

5. Optimisation 3: no Jacobian, one pass, in parallel

The 30 scalars need only gx, gy, x, y, ī, t̄ -- six planes . The Jacobian never has to be written, and never has to be read back.

Data flow, one iteration

Left: the current code. The Jacobian is materialised and re-read by four projections. Right: fused. Six planes are read once.

5.1 The inner loop

Accumulate in float per row and promote to double at the end of each row. It is straight-line code, so the compiler can vectorise it, and the per-row promotion keeps the precision.

for (int x = 0; x < w; ++x) {
    const float a = pgx[x], b = pgy[x], u = pX[x], v = pY[x];
    const float aa = a*a, ab = a*b, bb = b*b;   // the 3 entries of g g^T
    const float uu = u*u, uv = u*v, vv = v*v;   // the 6 entries of c c^T (u, v, 1 are trivial)

    g00 += aa*uu;  g01 += aa*uv;  g02 += aa*vv;   // gx^2 row
    g03 += aa*u;   g04 += aa*v;   g05 += aa;
    g10 += ab*uu;  g11 += ab*uv;  g12 += ab*vv;   // gxgy row
    g13 += ab*u;   g14 += ab*v;   g15 += ab;
    g20 += bb*uu;  g21 += bb*uv;  g22 += bb*vv;   // gy^2 row
    g23 += bb*u;   g24 += bb*v;   g25 += bb;

    const float ii = pim[x], tt = ptm[x];
    const float ai = a*ii, bi = b*ii, at = a*tt, bt = b*tt;
    pi0 += ai*u; pi1 += bi*u; pi2 += ai*v; pi3 += bi*v; pi4 += ai; pi5 += bi;
    pt0 += at*u; pt1 += bt*u; pt2 += at*v; pt3 += bt*v; pt4 += at; pt5 += bt;
}

6 multiplies + 18 FMA + 4 multiplies + 12 FMA per pixel, about 40 operations . Looping naively over 21 j[p]*j[q] with double costs 72 operations instead, and a nested loop carrying k++ does not vectorise (measured 2.4-2.8x slower).

5.2 Parallelism and the reduction

class GNBody : public ParallelLoopBody {
    void operator()(const Range& r) const CV_OVERRIDE {
        GNSums s;
        gnRows(gx_, gy_, X_, Y_, im_, tm_, r.start, r.end, s);  // row stripe
        AutoLock lk(mtx_);
        out_.push_back(s);                                       // only 30 scalars come back
    }
};

Only 30 double cross thread boundaries, so the reduction is effectively free. This matters because Mat::dot is not parallelised, and as it stands this stage uses one core out of twelve .

Three things are worth reading here.
  • The identity in 3 is cheap but small.1.01-1.10x for every motion type and window -- a fair return for a three-line change. It gives the same on a single thread, having nothing to do with parallelism.
  • The claim in 4 that the diagonal only needs dot turned out to be worthless.0.96-1.04x -- inside measurement noise, and it sometimes goes negative . It brings nothing but complexity, so it is not adopted.
  • The parallelism in 5 pays only when P is large. 1.20-1.29x for affine and homography, 1.03-1.10x for translation and euclidean. The two motion types with w=(1) have little projection work to begin with. On a single thread all of them fall to 0.96-1.04x, as they must.
The product of the parts (C1+C2+C3 measures 1.05-1.44x) does not reach the whole (1.07-2.50x). Most of what fusion gains comes from not materialising the Jacobian, rather than from adding up the parts.

7. What was tried and did not work

7.1 gemm makes it worse

The obvious idea -- lay the Jacobian out contiguously as P×N and take gemm with GTG so the block re-reads disappear -- is 2 to 3 times slower than the current code .

The gemm in OpenCV 4.1.0 is not tuned for the extremely tall and extremely wide shapes P×N and N×P . Stacking [G;i‾; t‾] and producing everything with a single AAT -- mathematically the prettiest option -- was slow in the same way.

7.2 Naive fusion is slower on a single thread

j[6] is built, and a nested loop over 21 j[p]*j[q] with double is 2.3 to 1.8 times slower than the current code. Factorising into straight-line code is the point; fusing on its own is not what makes it fast.

8. What is left, and what constrains it

  • The coordinates x, y do not have to be read. They are the column and row index, so they can be generated in place. Six planes become four, which should cut traffic by roughly a third (not implemented, not measured).
  • Explicit SIMD.Auto-vectorisation is doing the work at the moment. Thirty accumulators do not fit in sixteen YMM registers, so they spill to L1. Blocking should help.
  • Homography is implemented, and it gained the most.The first version of this report said here that the per-pixel division stops it being a face-splitting product. That was wrong. The division folds into the gradient side (4.3), and OpenCV's own image_jacobian_homo_ECC is already in that form. P = 8 has the most headroom, and measures 2.47× at window 768 on four threads -- the best of all four motion types.
  • translation and euclidean are implemented too, but gain little. w=(1) means the Hessian has only 3 or 6 entries, so this stage is a small share of the work. Window 768 on four threads: 1.27x and 1.88x. Euclidean still gets close to affine (2.00x) because building gr folds into the same pass.
  • Optimisation 1 is what goes upstream first.The linear combination in 3 is three lines, touches neither layout nor algorithm, and helps every motion type. Sections 4 and 5 introduce a new kernel and belong in a separate change.

9. Summary

  1. The projection of the update falls out of the algebra. GT(λ t‾− i‾)= λvt− vi . A three-line substitution frees 15 planes.
  2. For all four motion types the Jacobian is a column subset of a face-splitting product. What separates translation, euclidean, affine and homography is the contents of u, w and which columns are taken; the number of scalars accumulated is always tri(K)·tri(L) . For affine that is 18 rather than 21. H=∑n (gngnT) ⊗ (cncnT) follows immediately from the form.
  3. Without materialising the Jacobian, 85 planes per iteration become 6. Accumulating 30 scalars in one pass is all it takes.
  4. As it stands this stage uses one core (Mat::dot has no parallel_for_). Parallelising over row stripes is effectively free, the reduction being 30 scalars.
  5. The saving grows with P .The gain from not materialising the Jacobian is that one pass suffices, so more parameters means more saving. Window 768 on four threads: translation 1.27x / euclidean 1.88x / affine 2.00x / homography 2.47× . The first version of this report said homography was not implemented because it is not a face-splitting product; that was wrong, and it turned out to be the best case of all.
  6. Combined with the warp reduction there is no gap left.The warp reduction pays at small windows (1.26-1.52x at 256) and the fused stage at large ones, so with both, four threads gives 1.40-2.89x regardless of window or motion type . This stage taken alone reaches 6.3x. Accuracy, bias and convergence radius are all numerically unchanged.
