The warp reduction on top of the border fix, and re-tuning gaussFiltSize

Measured 2026-08-23. Subject: OpenCV cv::findTransformECC and opencv/opencv#29775 (the pre-filter border fix), together with fast-ecc (three warps per iteration cut to one)
Implementation eval/uni_ecc.cpp / raw data eval/results/ / Markdown version ECC_warp_gauss_revisit.md

0. The questions, and the answers

(a) Do they compose?

Yes

No loss of accuracy and none of convergence radius. At windows of 128-256 px, 1.24-1.49x.

(a) On large images it

does not pay

The gain is gone above a 384 px window, and 768-1024 px is break-even. The cause is an asymmetry in OpenCV threading (see 2.5).On a single thread it is 1.27-1.31x across the whole range.

(a) Accuracy is

best combined

The scale bias left in fast-ecc alone (t = -38) disappears once the two are combined.

(b) The best gauss

5 → 9

Accuracy holds (0.0016 -> 0.0017 px) while the convergence rate goes from 68% to 72%. Before the fix, 5 -> 9 cost a factor of 2.4 in accuracy.

(b) What the blur buys

Convergence radius, only

Under noise, more blur is strictly worse. It does not buy robustness to noise.

The goal was to measure and conclude.Nothing was adopted into an implementation and nothing further was sent upstream.

1. How this was measured

1.1 Removing the confound: one source, two flags

cand_ecc.cpp (the border fix) and fast_ecc.cpp (the warp reduction) derive from different upstream snapshots, so comparing them directly mixes in differences that belong to neither change. eval/uni_ecc.cpp gives one body two boolean flags and produces all four combinations from the same code path.

estimatorfixBorder fastWarpcorresponds to
ubase——unmodified upstream
ufix✓—opencv#29775
ufast—✓fast-ecc
uboth✓✓both (the subject of this report)

Agreement with the existing implementations was verified: at eval fixedpoint est=cv,ubase,cand,ufix,fast,ufast motion=affine , ubase=cv and ufix=cand match ufast=fast on cornMed, bias and rho to four decimal places.

The border fix is written in the same shape as the upstream PR: outside the loop the templtMask ring is dropped, and inside the loop the mask is intersected and gradients outside it are zeroed. The research copy bitwise_and rebuilds the ring every iteration, which would load the speed comparison with cost that belongs to neither change. cand_ecc.cpp

1.2 Speed is measured with the iteration count pinned

ms column is wall time for one call, so when estimators converge in different numbers of iterations the per-iteration cost is invisible.eps=0 makes the stopping test |rho - rho'| >= 0 always true, so every estimator runs iters iterations exactly. That is the per-iteration cost. Wall time to convergence was measured separately with the default eps=1e-6.

Median of three repeats per cell. Order effects were checked at window 256 by swapping the order (none found). A single measurement produced a spurious 48% difference, so repeats are mandatory.

The speed figures were re-taken with the machine quiet. The first measurements were contaminated by other heavy work on the same machine and were inflated by a factor of 1.4-1.6 (per-iteration cost, affine window 512: 178 ms contaminated against 129 ms re-measured). The trend in the ratios did not change, but individual cells moved by up to 0.14 (wall time to convergence, translation window 256: 1.59x -> 1.45x; per-iteration cost, affine window 128: 1.16x -> 1.27x). The re-measurement records CPU load before and after every repeat (the C1b/C2b lines in @@@@), and it stays within 0-35%.

1.3 Parallelism

ECC on a 200 px window keeps barely one core busy per process. Two sequential batches on a 12-core machine leave it 17% utilised, so the accuracy runs were split one process per configuration and fanned out 17 ways. Only the speed measurements were run alone.

That applies to a 200 px window. As 2.5 shows, warpAffine does reach the thread pool above 384 px, so at large windows ECC uses several cores too.To pin the thread count, use threads=N; — OPENCV_FOR_THREADS_NUM is ignored by this build.

Common settings: analytic scene (exact ground truth), window=200, margin=8, gaussFiltSize=5, trials=200-300, seed=12345.

  • uboth is as accurate as ufix, and it is.The warp reduction takes nothing away from what the border fix achieved.
  • ufast left a systematic scale shrink (t = -38), and uboth clears it. fast-ecc erodes the input mask by 2 px and the warped mask by 1 px, which happened to remove most of the pre-filter ring (2 px at gaussFiltSize=5). That is why the -2.8e-4 of ubase had shrunk ninefold to -3.1e-5 without ever reaching zero. It reaches zero only once the ring is dropped deliberately, in uboth. The mechanism is confirmed in 2.1.1.
  • All four agree on translation. The rings on opposite edges cancel, so the border has no effect and the value sits on the floor set by quantisation of the warp interpolation coefficients (about 0.011-0.018 px).

Starting 1 px off the ground truth, accuracy gives the same ordering (affine: 0.0406 / 0.0017 / 0.0051 / 0.0017).

2.1.1 The apparent accuracy of fast-ecc depended on gaussFiltSize=5 (mechanism confirmed)

The guess above -- that fast-ecc happened to drop most of the blur ring because it erodes the mask -- is testable. The ring is gaussFiltSize/2 wide, so sweeping gauss makes a prediction. fast-ecc erodes 1 px of the warped mask, so a 1 px ring (gauss=3) should be covered exactly and leave zero bias, and anything wider should leak.

As predicted. The mechanism is settled. Two things follow:

  • The accuracy advantage of fast-ecc alone held at gaussFiltSize=5 (the default) by coincidence, and is not a property of the algorithm. At gauss=17 it is only twice as good as ubase (0.2586).
  • So if 3 raises gaussFiltSize, the border fix is mandatory. The warp reduction alone cannot survive a wide blur.

2.5 Cause: an asymmetry in OpenCV threading (identified)

Correction.The first version of this report concluded here that threading was not the cause and that memory bandwidth was the likely explanation. That control used OPENCV_FOR_THREADS_NUM=1, but this OpenCV build ignores that environment variable (found by calling cv::setNumThreads directly: with the variable set, getNumThreads() still reports 12). So the comparison was multi-threaded against multi-threaded, and was no control at all. The conclusion is the opposite.

eval was given a threads=N option (which calls cv::setNumThreads) and the measurement redone. affine, trials=12, median of three repeats:

threads=4 column was re-measured later on affine (per-iteration cost, minimum of three repeats). Four threads is taken as the everyday operating point -- this machine is 6C/12T, but running 12 wide is outside realistic use. It behaves in between, and the loss of gain above window 512 follows the same trend as 12 threads.

On a single thread the warp reduction is completely flat at 1.27-1.31x from window 128 to 768. The degradation happens only when the thread pool is involved.

Broken down by operation, the reason fits in one line (eval/opbench.cpp, -tN pins the thread count). The numbers are one-thread time divided by twelve-thread time, that is, parallel speed-up:

2.6 Implication: at large windows the warp is no longer the main cost

There is a sequel to this mechanism. At window 768 on 12 threads, the two warps that can be removed are 1.12 ms (7%) of a 16.2 ms iteration. Even a perfectly fused and parallelised replacement would cap out around 1.06x.

At large windows there is no headroom left on the gradient path.The remaining nine tenths is the Gauss-Newton algebra: Jacobian assembly and project_onto_jacobian_ECC, eight planes for homography. On a single thread the warp is 18% of an iteration instead, so the warp reduction pays directly.
Added later: this prediction was implemented and confirmed.Fusing the Gauss-Newton stage does take that headroom (ECC_gauss_newton_fusion.html). At four threads and window 768, the fused stage alone gives translation 1.27x / euclidean 1.88x / affine 2.00x / homography 2.47x. It is exactly complementary to the region where the warp reduction is powerless.

What this means in practice:

  • cv::setNumThreads(1) (embedded use, or parallelism applied outside), the warp reduction gives 1.27-1.31x at every image size.
  • With a thread pool and a window above 384 px, the warp reduction is worth almost nothing. It does no harm either (break-even), but expect nothing from it.The fused Gauss-Newton stage fills that region.
  • Below a 384 px window it gives 1.25-1.5x regardless of thread count.
  • Taking four threads as the everyday operating point, the warp reduction gives 1.26-1.52x at window 256 and 1.04-1.17x at window 768. It does not fall off as sharply as at 12 threads.

4. Conclusions, and what comes next

Settled

  1. The warp reduction sits on top of the border fix unchanged.No loss of accuracy or convergence radius, and 1.24-1.49x faster at windows of 128-256 px. The scale bias left in fast-ecc alone disappears when the two are combined, so the combination is also the most accurate.
  2. The accuracy advantage of fast-ecc alonegaussFiltSize=5 was a coincidence that depended on gaussFiltSize=5 (2.1.1). It erodes the mask by only 1 px, so the ring is covered exactly when it is 1 px wide (gauss=3) and not otherwise. At gauss=9 the residual bias is back to -1.8e-04 (t=-75), and at gauss=17 to -7.4e-04 (t=-51).
  3. The speed-up disappears above a 384 px window (0.98-1.03x at 768-1024 px, effectively break-even). It never clearly gets slower.
  4. The cause is an asymmetry in OpenCV threading, and it is identified (2.5).warpAffine reaches the thread pool above 384 px (up to 4.5x), while filter2D/addWeighted never does. On a single thread the saving is strictly proportional to pixel count (dt/Mpx stays at 6.8-7.5 across a 64-fold change in pixel count), and the ratio is flat at 1.27-1.31x from 128 to 768 px. Note that the first version of this report wrongly concluded the opposite here -- the control it used, OPENCV_FOR_THREADS_NUM, is ignored by this build.
  5. At large windows the warp is no longer the main cost.At window 768 on 12 threads, 1.12 ms of a 16.2 ms iteration (7%) is removable warp. Even a perfect fused and parallelised replacement caps out near 1.06x; the other nine tenths is the Gauss-Newton algebra.
  6. The border fix is speed-neutral.There is no performance objection to the upstream PR.
  7. gaussFiltSize moves from 5 to 9 (same accuracy, 4 more points of convergence rate). Before the fix, 5 was close to the ceiling of a compromise.
  8. The pre-filter does not buy robustness to noise.What it buys is convergence radius, and nothing else.

Not attempted

  • Improving large windows means the Gauss-Newton algebra (2.6). Fusing and parallelising the gradient path caps out near 1.06x at window 768 on 12 threads. The place to work is project_onto_jacobian_ECC instead.
  • A fused kernel for the added side.filter2D × 2 + addWeighted × 2 + setToFolding parallel_for_ into a single pass (ten planes read and written down to three) would at least make the 384-768 px trough shallower. It does nothing for single-thread users, who already have 1.27x.
  • gaussFiltSize Proposing a change of default upstream.3.2 is grounds for it, but changing a default changes behaviour for existing users. Better not to raise it until #29775 is merged.
  • The residual bias at small windows with large gauss (+4.9e-05, t=4.0). The sign is reversed, so the mechanism is different.
  • Reproduction on real images (synth=resample).Everything here is the analytic scene. Its advantage is exact ground truth; the price is a monotonous band (freq=0.22, 40 components).

cd eval
cmake -S . -B build -DOpenCV_DIR=C:/opencv/build/x64/vc15/lib
